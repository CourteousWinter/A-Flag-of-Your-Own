# Game vision

## The player fantasy

Build a life at sea, from an unknown deckhand to a captain with a ship, a loyal crew, and eventually a fleet. Success creates new ways to live: a better cabin, influence in port, an island home, and the possibility of directing voyages from an estate overlooking the harbor.

The life aboard the ship matters as much as the ship itself. Daily work, relationships, personal choices, and the places the player calls home should connect the early career to the later command of a fleet.

## Progression direction

| Stage | Player focus |
| --- | --- |
| Deckhand | Learn shipboard work, earn pay, and get to know the crew. |
| Trusted sailor | Take on responsibilities and build skills, savings, and reputation. |
| Captain | Command a ship, recruit a crew, choose voyages, and manage supplies. |
| Fleet owner | Acquire ships, appoint captains, and coordinate expeditions. |
| Island estate owner | Develop a home and direct maritime affairs from shore. |

These stages describe the intended fantasy. Their order, unlock conditions, and pacing still need design and playtesting.

## Proposed core loop

Work and live aboard a ship, build skills and relationships, complete voyages, earn money and reputation, and invest in greater independence. Each investment should introduce meaningful choices and new responsibilities.

## Decisions still open

- Player perspective and camera.
- Historical setting, fictional setting, and degree of fantasy.
- Single-player and any multiplayer ambitions.
- Balance between simulation, exploration, trade, and combat.
- Whether naval work offers alternative careers alongside piracy.

## First playable target

Prove a short deckhand experience on one stationary ship: move around, accept a chore, finish it, receive pay and reputation, and save that progress. Expand to sailing and ownership after that loop is enjoyable.
